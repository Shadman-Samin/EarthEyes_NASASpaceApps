Observe first. Explain carefully. Warn only when verified.

Radar change can be caused by water, vegetation, soil moisture, tides, farming or imaging geometry. EarthEyes separates what the sensor saw from what analysts infer and what authorities choose to communicate.

02 / FROM PIXELS TO PEOPLE

Warnings should reach the whole village.

GroundLine is the last-mile idea: simple, location-specific SMS or voice alerts for basic phones. No app, account or data plan. A real deployment would only send warnings after cross-checking satellite signals with trusted forecast and local-authority sources.

03 / HOW A SIGNAL BECOMES A WARNING

Four checks between space and shore.

The satellite is one input in a warning chain—not a siren by itself.

01 / OBSERVERadar sees a change

NISAR L-band / S-band observations help reveal surface change through clouds and darkness.

02 / CROSS-CHECKBring in local context

Compare with rainfall, river gauges, tide, forecasts, terrain and time-stamped community reports.

03 / VALIDATEAnalyst + authority review

Check quality, uncertainty, location and thresholds with responsible agencies and local partners.

04 / REACH PEOPLESMS or voice in plain words

Send a relevant action message to affected areas, then update or cancel it as conditions change.

04 / START WITH A PLACE

Local stories, not abstract pixels.

Use a guided case study to move from broad geography to a specific place and a useful question.
